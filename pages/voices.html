🎙️ Voices of Kumbalangi

The stories that matter most come from the people who have lived here for decades.

A Lifetime by the Backwaters

Conversation with a 72-year-old resident

He has watched Kumbalangi change across generations — from a quieter fishing village to a place that now receives visitors drawn by cinema, nature and the promise of glowing waters.

What has changed?

  • Fishing then vs now Traditional fishing is less common. Many younger people have moved into other work.
  • Village life Daily rhythms have shifted with new roads, education and tourism.
  • Employment More young people choose jobs outside traditional occupations.
  • Tourism Tourism brings income but also changes how the village is experienced.
  • Water & environment Concerns about reduced water depth and accumulation of mud/silt in parts of the waterways.

Kumbalangi through his eyes

“This is far more powerful than simply writing ‘Kumbalangi has fishing.’ His memories give the place a human timeline.”

A Lifetime by the Backwaters

Conversation with a Local Vendor

Chinese fishing nets and Kavaru are the main attractions of the area. While some residents continue traditional fishing, many have shifted to daily-wage jobs in cities, and younger generations are exploring alternative livelihoods. Tourism provides valuable business opportunities for locals; however, tourists often litter and disturb fishing areas and ecosystems, especially during the Kavaru season. The interviewee highlighted the need for eco-friendly and sustainable tourism practices to protect the local environment while supporting the community's economy and preserving traditional fishing practices.
