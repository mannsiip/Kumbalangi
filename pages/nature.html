🌿 Nature of Kumbalangi

Mangroves, backwaters, aquatic life and the glow that has drawn the world.

🌱 Mangroves

Mangrove forests form a critical living edge between land and water in Kumbalangi. Bait fishing near the mangroves is popular among tourists.

  • Poomeen Chattam, the movement of a group of fish in half circle during high tide, is a visual delight that you will enjoy.
  • They act as breeding grounds for crabs, prawns, oysters and small fishes.
  • They help protect shorelines and support the wider aquatic ecosystem.
  • Kerala Tourism recognises Kumbalangi’s mangrove areas as important habitats.

Why visitors should protect them: Walking into or damaging mangrove zones harms the very biodiversity that makes the backwaters productive.

✨ Kavaru

When the backwaters glow

Sea sparkle or bioluminescence, locally known as ‘kavaru’ is a unique attraction along the backwaters of Kumbalangi. The ‘magical glow’ is caused by the high concentration of a micro-plankton called Noctiluca Scintillans in the sea.

⚠️
Can you see it anytime?

No. Bioluminescence is a natural phenomenon. Its occurrence varies with environmental conditions, salinity, season and light. It is more commonly reported in warmer months (often February–May in recent years), but never guaranteed. Do not disturb the water or ecosystem just to create the effect.

🌊 Why does the water glow?

Micro-organisms → movement or disturbance → chemical reaction that produces a blue-green glow.

Please remember

Do not disturb the water or the ecosystem simply to force the glow. Respect farms, private areas and the organisms themselves.
